## 6. Business Fundamentals and Long-Term Trend

### Dividend funding engine

The dividend is funded by **domestic connectivity subscription cash flow** — 439m mobile and 201m broadband subscribers paying monthly fees into a protected three-operator oligopoly. That base (RMB 330.5bn, 68% of service revenue) is the cash engine. The industrial-digitalisation/cloud arm (RMB 147.3bn) is the designated *growth* engine — and it has stalled.

### Fundamental Trend classification: **Stable / Mature (with a permanent FY2026 earnings reset)**

**Why not Structural Decline:** revenue is flat rather than shrinking; EBITDA still grew +2.1% in FY2025; the franchise is a licensed, state-protected oligopoly with no substitution threat to the asset base; capex is falling and free cash flow is rising; the balance sheet is net cash. The FY2026 profit fall is a **one-step level reset from a tax change**, not a compounding erosion — after 2026 the comparison base normalises.

**Why not Stable alone:** the growth engine management pointed investors to (industrial digitalisation, cloud) decelerated from +17.9% to +0.5% in two years, cash conversion is deteriorating through receivables, and the state demonstrated in January 2026 that it will change the economics of the business without notice or compensation.

### Core forecast drivers (3–5)

1. **Connectivity revenue** — subscribers near saturation; ARPU roughly RMB 45–46/month for mobile and RMB 47/month for broadband, flat to slightly down. *(Source conflict: FY2025 mobile ARPU reported as RMB 45.1 by one source and RMB 46.0 by another; both imply ±1% around flat.)*
2. **VAT reclassification (the dominant FY2026 driver)** — from 1 Jan 2026 mobile data, SMS/MMS and broadband internet access moved from "value-added telecom" (6% VAT) to "basic telecom" (9% VAT) under MOF/STA Announcement 2026 No. 9. Since revenue is booked net of VAT, an unchanged retail price cuts recognised revenue by 1.06/1.09 − 1 = **−2.75%** on affected lines. On an estimated RMB 230–260bn affected base that is **−RMB 6.3 to −7.2bn of revenue and EBITDA**, and **−RMB 4.7 to −5.4bn of net profit (−14% to −16%)** — matching broker estimates of −13% to −16%. Repricing to recover it is effectively blocked by MIIT tariff-reduction policy and competition.
3. **Industrial digitalisation / cloud growth** — the swing factor for whether FY2027–28 recovers or stagnates.
4. **Capex** — guided **RMB 73.0bn for FY2026** (−9.2%), of which RMB 25.5bn to computing/AI infrastructure. Falling capex is the main reason FCF holds up despite lower profit.
5. **Working capital / receivables** — 1H2025 receivables rose RMB 19.4bn and OCF fell RMB 11.3bn; Q1 2026 impairments were a stated cause of the profit miss. Growth in government/enterprise digitalisation is being bought with credit.

### Per-share effects

Share count has been **flat at 91.507bn since the Aug 2021 A-share IPO**. No scrip, no DRIP, no buyback, no issuance. Per-share economics therefore move one-for-one with aggregate economics — a genuine positive versus HK peers that dilute through scrip.

### **The structural point this bridge reveals**

Cash Available for Distribution (**RMB 42bn Base**) is roughly **twice** the dividend the policy will actually pay (**RMB 21.75bn**). China Telecom's dividend is **constrained by accounting profit, not by cash**. The consequence is asymmetric and easy to misread:

- The dividend is very unlikely to *fail* — cash cover is ~2x and the balance sheet is net cash.
- But the dividend will still be *cut* in FY2026, because a policy of "75% of net profit" transmits a profit reset directly into DPS regardless of how much cash is available.

An investor buying on "the cash flow easily covers it" will still take a ~12% income cut.
