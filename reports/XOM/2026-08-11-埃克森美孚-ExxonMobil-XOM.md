**Interpretation.** Two distinct conclusions must be kept separate:

1. **The ordinary dividend has been funded by recurring operating free cash flow every year since 2021**, with coverage never below 1.37x. It was *not* funded by debt, equity issuance, or asset sales. This is a genuinely well-covered dividend.

That well is now largely dry: cash sat at US$10.6bn at 30 June 2026, which is at or near the group's working minimum.

The practical implication is that **the buyback, not the dividend, is the shock absorber.** At a mid-cycle Brent of $75 the company generates roughly US$26.5bn of FCF, of which US$17.4bn goes to the dividend, leaving ~US$9bn — well short of the US$20bn/yr repurchase pace. Either oil stays above ~$85, or the buyback halves. Income investors should read this as *protective* of the dividend (there is a large discretionary buffer above it) but *negative* for the per-share compounding that currently supports the share price.

The argument is that incremental capital should not be added at this price, and that a holder whose objective is after-tax cash income is currently being paid 1.8% to carry full commodity-cycle risk.
