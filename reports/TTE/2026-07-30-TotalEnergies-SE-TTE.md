## 4. Company and Listing Structure

*The single most under-appreciated fact here: TTE on the NYSE is no longer an ADR — it is a French ordinary share, and that materially improves the tax and fee picture for a HK holder.*

- **Domicile / issuer type:** French *société anonyme*, headquartered Paris. Ordinary share — not a REIT, trust, fund, MLP or partnership.
- **Listing structure:** TotalEnergies **terminated its ADR programme on 30 October 2025, effective 8 December 2025**. Each ADR was exchanged 1-for-1 for an ordinary share now listed directly on the NYSE (CUSIP F92124 100). The NYSE and Euronext Paris lines are a **single class of fully fungible shares** with identical voting and economic rights. Settlement is T+1 on NYSE vs T+2 in Paris, so the NYSE record date falls one business day earlier.
- **Practical consequences:** no depositary bank in the chain, **no ADR custody or dividend pass-through fee**, and no depositary FX spread layered on top. TotalEnergies states it levies no dividend fee.
- **Reporting currency:** USD. **Dividend declaration currency:** EUR. **Payment currency:** USD on the NYSE line, EUR on the Euronext line. NYSE payment lands ~2-3 weeks later than Euronext.
- **Share count (from filings):** 2,167m fully diluted at 31 Dec 2025 (2,214m weighted average during 2025); 26.3m shares repurchased in H1 2026 → **≈2,140m currently**. Third-party sites (Yahoo, Stockopedia) show ~2,224m, which appears to be a gross/pre-cancellation count — the filing figure is used throughout.

## 8. Management Capital Allocation

*Clear, quantified, publicly restated policy with a visible hierarchy — dividend first, buyback second, balance sheet third.*

- **Dividend policy:** priority given to the dividend and its growth through cycles. Interims raised annually; FY2026 interim +5.9% to €0.90.
- **Shareholder return policy:** **>40% of CFFO returned to shareholders regardless of energy prices**, where payout = (dividends + buybacks for cancellation) / CFFO excluding working capital. Delivered 37.2% (2022), 46.0% (2023), 50% (2024), 55% (2025), 33% in H1 2026.
- **Buyback policy:** explicitly the flexible component. 2026 guidance is **$3–6bn for the year** ($0.75–1.5bn/quarter) at Brent $60–70 and EUR/USD ~1.20 — cut from $2bn/quarter in 2025. Actual: $0.75bn in Q1, $1.5bn in Q2, $1.5bn authorised for Q3.
- **Reinvestment:** net investments guided to **~$15bn in 2026** (down from $17.1bn in 2025), of which ~$3–4bn low-carbon. Targets ~3% oil & gas production growth and ~25% electricity growth (>60 TWh), with ~$10bn of FCF growth by 2030 vs 2024 at constant prices.
- **Leverage:** gearing 13.1% at 30 June 2026, net debt $19.7bn against $27.7bn of cash and ~$118bn of equity. No refinancing pressure.
- **Share issuance / ATM:** none. No equity issuance concurrent with elevated payout.

**The one blemish:** running a 55% payout while gearing doubled in 2025 was a discretionary stretch. The policy floor is 40%; they went to 55%. It was corrected quickly, and the correction fell on the buyback rather than the dividend — which is exactly what an income holder wants to see — but it does show the payout ratio is managed toward optics as well as toward cash.

## 9. Buyback Quality

*Real, large, and it actually shrank the share count — the rarest of the three.*

- **Share count:** 2,640m (end-2021) → 2,619m (2022) → 2,412m (2023) → 2,167m fully diluted (end-2025) → ~2,140m today. That is a **~19% reduction in under five years**, with 122.6m shares (~5.4%) retired in 2025 alone and 26.3m in H1 2026.
- **Dilution:** negligible. No offsetting issuance, no ATM, no scrip dividend.
- **Valuation discipline:** the bulk of the 2022–2025 repurchases were executed at €50–65, below today's €75.68. Retrospectively good execution. The 2026 slowdown at a higher price is, if anything, evidence of price sensitivity.
- **Funding:** partly debt-funded in 2024–2025 (the $5.4bn 2025 gap). This is the only real mark against it.
- **Effect on the dividend:** each 5% of share count retired reduces the euro cash cost of the dividend by 5%, which is a large part of why DPS can grow 5-6% a year while total dividend cash outflow grows only ~2-3%.

**Rating: Good** — genuine per-share accretion at reasonable prices, docked for the debt-funded portion.

**Current price is ABOVE the income entry zone.** €75.68 is 8.5% above the top of the fair-value band (€69.76) and 27–35% above the accumulation and strong-buy boundaries.

The zone is disciplined but not unreachable — the stock traded inside the strong-buy band as recently as late 2025 / early 2026.

**What would move the view to buy:** Brent settling back to $60–70 with the geopolitical premium unwinding, taking TTE toward €55–62 ($63–71) — the fair-value band and a reasonable place to start. Below €54.60 ($62.70), accumulate methodically. The single realistic catalyst is a durable Middle East de-escalation, which mechanically compresses the oil price and the share price without touching the €0.90 quarterly interim at all. That is the setup worth waiting for: an income asset whose price falls for reasons that do not damage its income.
