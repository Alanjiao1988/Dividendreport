## 6. Business Fundamentals and Long-Term Trend

### Dividend funding engine

GSK's dividend is funded by **group free cash flow**, dominated by two engines:

1. **Specialty Medicines** (FY2025 £13.5bn, +17%) — HIV via ViiV (£7.7bn, +11%), Oncology (£2.0bn, +43%), Respiratory/Immunology & Inflammation (£3.8bn, +18%). This is the growth engine.
2. **Vaccines** (FY2025 £9.2bn, +2%) — Shingrix £3.6bn (+8%), Meningitis £1.6bn (+12%), Arexvy £0.6bn (+2%). High margin but demand has been soft in the US.

**General Medicines** (£10.0bn, −1%) is a mature cash cow (Trelegy £3.0bn, +13%) in structural decline at the segment level, and is being deliberately de-emphasised.

Two cash-flow leakages matter and are already deducted inside GSK's reported FCF:
- **Shionogi contingent consideration** — royalty-like payments tied to dolutegravir/cabotegravir sales, **£710m in H1 2026 alone** (H1 2025 £650m), paid through *operating* cash flow. Total contingent consideration liability £6,781m at 30 Jun 2026.
- **NCI distributions** — £391m (FY25), £416m (FY24), £412m (FY23), largely ViiV minorities.

> **Key insight for the LoE risk:** because Shionogi payments **scale with dolutegravir sales**, they fall away as dolutegravir erodes. This is a genuine ~£1.4bn/yr natural cash-flow hedge that materially cushions FCF versus the revenue decline. Combined with £1.9bn of announced cost savings by 2029, GSK's claim of a "stable to improving operating margin through the LoE period" is **credible rather than promotional**.

GSK discloses that **long-acting medicines contributed over 75% of total HIV growth in 2025**, and that **long-acting injectables now account for 30% of total US HIV sales**. The long-acting franchise is built on **cabotegravir, a different molecule protected to 2031 in both the US and EU** — three years beyond the dolutegravir cliff. Growing at +46% off a £1.84bn base, it plausibly reaches £4.5–5.5bn by 2030 on its own momentum, against a dolutegravir-exposed base of roughly £5.8bn (~18% of group revenue). The pipeline behind it (CAB-ULA four-monthly dosing, VH184 third-generation INSTI, VH310 cabotegravir pro-drug, VH499 capsid inhibitor, N6LS broadly neutralising antibody) is explicitly framed by GSK as the means to "navigate dolutegravir's loss of exclusivity towards the end of the decade."

**Net assessment:** the cliff is real and large, but it is (i) well-flagged and precisely dated, (ii) partly self-hedging through the Shionogi contingent-consideration mechanism, (iii) offset by a fast-growing, longer-dated cabotegravir franchise, and (iv) supported by £1.9bn of cost savings landing by 2029. This is why the classification is **Stable / Mature with a normalization window**, not Structural Decline.

**Already-live pricing headwind:** the IRA Medicare Part D redesign is **not a future risk — it is already reducing net prices**. The 20-F attributes 2025 pressure specifically to Zejula (US sales down on "unfavourable pricing including the impacts of IRA Medicare Part D redesign"), Nucala ("ongoing pricing pressures including the impact of IRA Medicare Part D redesign"), and HIV (offset in 2025 by favourable US channel mix).

### Reconciling the conflict between methods (required disclosure)

This framework values GSK **solely on after-tax dividend income**, and on that basis 1,960.5p is unattractive. A different method reaches a different answer: at ~**10.8x FY2026E Core EPS** with mid-single-digit earnings growth, a reaffirmed >£40bn 2031 sales outlook, and Zantac litigation now largely resolved in GSK's favour, GSK is **not expensive on earnings** relative to global pharma peers.

The gap is entirely explained by **payout policy, not by dividend risk**: GSK distributes only ~38% of Core EPS. If GSK paid at its own policy midpoint of 50%, DPS would be ~92p and the forward yield at today's price would be **4.7%** — inside the Fair zone. Per `buy-zone.md` §13, this conflict is disclosed rather than resolved by force:

- For an investor whose objective is **current cash income**, GSK does not clear the bar at this price. Wait for ≤1,600p.
- For an investor whose objective is **total return**, GSK may well be attractive here — but that judgment requires a DCF/DDM or moat framework, which is **outside the scope of this skill**.

**Verified facts (reported):** all FY2021–FY2025 financials; FY2025 and H1 2026 results; declared DPS FY2022–FY2025; FY2026 DPS guidance of 70p; buyback execution (£2,011m / 124m shares); Nuvalent consideration and closing date; net debt; share counts; dividend policy wording; ADR depositary fee; ex/record/payment dates.

GSK explicitly states these assume risk-adjusted pipeline delivery, **no material further business development**, and **no premature loss of exclusivity** — assumptions the Nuvalent deal has already partly contradicted.

**Analyst estimates (mine, clearly labelled):** FY2027 and FY2028 revenue, Core OP, OCF, FCF, and DPS; the mid-cycle Core EPS of ~185p underpinning N; the Bear/Base/Bull scenario architecture; the deleveraging allocation in the distributable-cash bridge.

**Missing or unverified data — stated rather than invented:**
1. **No user broker statement for GSK.** Withholding basis is `legal_structure`, not `broker_observed`. GSK is **not** added to the User Observed Withholding Register. Confirm the actual IBKR cash-line treatment (dividend vs PIL) on the next payment before relying on the 0% assumption.
2. **US Generous Model / MFN terms are confidential** — GSK states this explicitly. The −3% US net price sensitivity is an estimate, not a modelled term.
3. **Erosion *depth* after patent expiry is estimated.** The expiry *dates* are company-disclosed (20-F patent tables); the pace and depth of generic penetration are not, and are the largest source of forecast error in FY2028.
4. **FY2023 Core OP (~£8.8bn) and Core EPS (~149p)** are derived from reported growth rates, not directly quoted.
5. **FY2021–FY2022 FCF is not shown** — the Haleon demerger (18 Jul 2022) makes those cash flows non-comparable. The coverage analysis deliberately uses only the clean post-demerger period (FY2023–FY2025).
6. **No third-party analyst consensus, credit-rating, or price-target cross-check was incorporated.** A supplementary research pass was run and **discarded**: it could not access Reuters, FT, Bloomberg, S&P, Moody's, or CMS (all returned 401/403/paywalls) and returned unverified recollection that **contradicted primary sources** on two material points — ViiV ownership (it claimed ~76.5%; the 20-F states GSK was entitled to **~83%** of ViiV earnings for 2025) and pro-forma leverage (it implied ~3x; Core EBITDA of ~£11bn+ gives **~1.9–2.0x**).
