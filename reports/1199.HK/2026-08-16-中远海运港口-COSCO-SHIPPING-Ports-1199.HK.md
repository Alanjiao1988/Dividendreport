### 6.1 The dividend funding engine — this is not a normal consolidated operator

## 9. Management Capital Allocation

| Dimension | Assessment |
|---|---|
| Payout policy | Clear, consistent, mechanically applied. 40.0% for five straight years. **But no numeric floor is codified** — the written policy only promises "stable dividend payout over the medium term", so the 40% can be changed by the Board |
| Reinvestment | Very heavy. Capex US$326m/yr average plus US$150.8m of equity-interest investments in FY2025 alone (acquisition of associates US$110.6m, JV and associate injections US$40.3m) |
| Leverage | Managed down well — net debt US$2,085m → US$1,895m; net gearing 29.6% → 25.1%; average borrowing cost 5.21% → 4.54% |
| Acquisitions | Continuous bolt-on stake purchases in terminals; LaemChabang commitment settled in 2025 |
| Ordinary issuance / ATM | None beyond scrip |
| Scrip / DRIP | **Persistent and unoffset.** ~200m new shares per year, ~5% of the register |
| Buybacks | **Never** |

**The core capital-allocation problem.** Combine a 40% payout with an ~83% scrip take-up and the company retains roughly **93% of its earnings** — 60% withheld by policy, plus 33 percentage points handed back as stock. Across FY2021–FY2025 that is on the order of **US$1.4bn of retained shareholder capital**. Over the same window, profit attributable went from US$354.7m to US$312.1m — **down US$42.5m**. The incremental return on retained earnings has been approximately **zero or negative**, and reported ROE has slipped to **5.1%** (from 5.3%). Some of this is timing — Chancay absorbed capital for years before generating revenue (US$1.7m in 2024 → US$57.1m in 2025) — but five years is long enough that it can no longer be dismissed as a build-out artefact.

**The scrip is also quietly transferring control.** Because the 72.27% parent elects shares while a majority of the free float takes cash, the parent's stake ratchets up at no cash cost. My reconstruction (validated against the actual share and dividend movements) puts it at **~71.5% at end-2024 → 72.27% at end-2025, roughly +0.7pp per year.** Public float is now **27.73%** against a **25% Listing Rule minimum** — about **2.7pp, or three to four years, of headroom**. The company already had to raise authorised share capital from 4.0bn to 5.0bn shares at the 2026 AGM because scrip issuance had consumed 99% of the old ceiling. The scrip machine has a visible expiry date around **2029–2030**, at which point either the scheme is curtailed (positive for cash income) or the parent must be restrained. This is a real, datable structural event and I have not seen it discussed in the results materials.

**Capital allocation score: 6 / 15.** The policy is clear and consistently honoured, which prevents the lowest band. Persistent unoffset scrip dilution explicitly bars the highest band, and the absent return on retained capital keeps it low.

## 10. Buyback Quality

**Not a single share has been repurchased in the period reviewed.** Meanwhile ~198.6m shares were issued in 2025 and ~197.8m in 2024. True diluted share count is rising 4–5.5% per year with no offset whatsoever.

This is the mirror image of a value-accretive buyback: the company is issuing equity at **0.40× book value** — arguably a worse deal for continuing holders than a repurchase at that price would be a good one. A repurchase programme funded from the FY2025 CAFD surplus would have been strongly per-share accretive at HK$5.015 and would have neutralised the dilution outright. Management has chosen not to.

**Buyback Quality: Poor — 2 / 10.**

The drivers interact — a weaker trade environment would plausibly depress profit, depress the share price, and *therefore increase* the number of scrip shares issued per dollar of dividend, compounding the dilution. That reflexivity is not captured in the one-at-a-time table.

**Note on the scrip sensitivity.** Over a three-year window, cutting take-up from 80% to 60% moves DPS less than 1% — the dilution compounds slowly. Over ten years the same change is worth roughly a fifth of DPS. **Scrip dilution is not a three-year swing factor; it is a decade-long erosion factor.** That is precisely why it belongs in the required yield rather than in the forecast.
