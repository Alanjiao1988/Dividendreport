### The mechanism — why an individual still pays 10%

The FY2025 dividend announcement states the company must:

> "…withhold and pay **10 per cent.** enterprise income tax on the distribution of the 2025 final dividend to **non-resident enterprise holders**… (including **HKSCC Nominees Limited**, other corporate nominees or trustees such as securities companies and banks…)"

The chain that matters:

1. You hold 0941.HK through IBKR. Your shares are **not** on China Mobile's register in your name.
2. They sit in a single block registered to **HKSCC Nominees Limited**.
3. The announcement **explicitly names HKSCC as a non-resident enterprise holder**.
4. China Mobile therefore withholds 10% on the entire HKSCC block **at source**, before the clearing chain distributes anything.
5. You receive the net amount. HKSCC does not pursue refunds for individual investors.

| Holder type | Registration | WHT applied | Rate |
|---|---|---|---:|
| Non-resident enterprise | Direct on register | Yes | 10% |
| Individual, direct on register | Direct on register | Typically not required | 0% |
| **HK individual via broker** | **Via HKSCC Nominees** | **Yes** | **10%** |

**Withholding Efficiency: Medium.** Better than a US ordinary share (30% for a HK resident); materially worse than a true HK-domiciled payer with no PRC nexus (0%). The 10% is a permanent, non-recoverable drag of roughly 63bp of yield at the current price.

**Caveat.** The announcements do not state a 10% rate *for individuals*; the 10% arises indirectly because HKSCC is the registered holder. This interpretation is consistent with the observed IBKR record and with market practice, but it is an interpretation. A formal PRC tax opinion would be the gold standard.

### Fundamental Trend classification: **Stable / Mature**

**Evidence for Stable / Mature (not Structural Growth):**
- Operating revenue growth has collapsed from +10.5% (FY2022) to **+0.9%** (FY2025). Q1 2026: +1.0%.
- Mobile subscribers are saturated: 957mn → 1,005mn over four years (+1.2%/yr) against a ~1.4bn population.
- Mobile ARPU is **falling** — RMB 46.8/month in FY2025 versus the ~RMB 52 range in FY2023 — under the government's long-running 提速降费 tariff-reduction policy.
- EBITDA margin has compressed 380bp, from ~36.1% (FY2021) to 32.3% (FY2025).
- FY2025 delivered the **first decline in profit attributable to shareholders (−0.9%)**, and Q1 2026 fell a further −4.2%.

**Evidence against Structural Decline:**
- Revenue is still growing, not shrinking.
- Balance sheet is net cash by RMB 130–276bn; there is no financing constraint.
- Capex is in structural decline (RMB 151bn FY2025, guided RMB 136.6bn FY2026), which mechanically *expands* free cash flow.
- Digital transformation revenue reached RMB 278.8bn (31.3% of service revenue) and computing services RMB 89.8bn (+11.1%) — a real, funded second engine.
- Regulated oligopoly with two state-owned peers; no destructive price war.

### Three core drivers

1. **Mobile ARPU (RMB 46.8/mth).** The single largest swing factor. Near-100% incremental margin, so ARPU moves hit profit almost undiluted. Direction is negative under tariff policy.
2. **Computing / digital services revenue (RMB 89.8bn, +11.1%).** The growth engine — but at ~15–20% operating margin versus telecom's ~30%+, so it takes roughly two RMB of cloud revenue to replace one RMB of lost telecom profit.
3. **Capex trajectory (RMB 150.9bn → 136.6bn guided).** Post-5G normalisation is the main reason free cash flow can rise while profit is flat. Within the 2026 envelope, computing-power capex is up 62.4% while radio-network capex falls.

### Per-share dilution

**Zero.** No scrip, no DRIP, no employee-option overhang of consequence, no equity issuance since the December 2021 A-share IPO (~RMB 56bn), and no buybacks. Share count has been flat at ~21,571mn. Every RMB of DPS growth is real per-share growth — a genuine, and increasingly rare, positive.

### Dividend Pattern

Six consecutive years of ordinary DPS growth with no cut, no suspension, no special dividends, and no scrip. On the surface, an exemplary record.

The decomposition tells a harder story:

- **DPS CAGR FY2021 → FY2025: +6.7%/yr** (HKD 4.06 → 5.27)
- **EPS CAGR FY2021 → FY2025: +2.9%/yr** (RMB 5.67 → 6.35)
- **The difference is entirely the payout ratio: 65.2% → 75.5%**

Roughly **57% of the last four years' dividend growth came from the payout ratio, not the business.** With the ratio now at 75% and management guiding only "stable-to-rising", the remaining headroom is perhaps 5 percentage points to ~80% — worth roughly one more 6–7% DPS step in aggregate. After that, **DPS growth mathematically converges to earnings growth of ~2–3%.**

This is not a criticism of management; lifting the payout of an over-capitalised, ex-growth utility is exactly correct capital allocation. But an investor extrapolating the historical 6.7% DPS CAGR into the future is extrapolating a non-repeatable input.

### Interpretation

For FY2021–FY2024, every dividend RMB came from recurring operating free cash flow. There is no debt-funded payout, no asset-sale funding, and no equity-funded payout anywhere in the record. The December 2021 A-share IPO raised capital *for the company*; it did not fund distributions.

**FY2025 is the exception and it deserves to be stated plainly.** Free cash flow of RMB 82.0bn fell short of the RMB 104.4bn paid out — a gap of ~RMB 22bn drawn from the cash balance. Three points frame it:

1. **The shortfall is trivially affordable.** Against RMB 233–345bn of cash and short-term investments and only ~RMB 101bn of debt, RMB 22bn is roughly one to two months of gross cash generation.
2. **The cause is on the OCF line, not the capex line.** Capex actually *fell* 8%. OCF dropped from ~RMB 315.7bn to ~RMB 232.9bn — a RMB 83bn swing that is far too large to be explained by the RMB 1.3bn change in net income. This points to working capital, and the plausible culprit is the mix shift into enterprise/digital business, which carries materially longer receivables than consumer prepaid telecom.
3. **The early evidence says timing, not structure.** Q1 2026 operating cash flow rose **+128% YoY**. That is consistent with a working-capital reversal.

**This analysis treats the FY2025 coverage break as most likely transient but explicitly unverified.** The primary FY2025 cash flow statement could not be read in the research for this report, and the H1 2026 interim (due ~14 Aug 2026) is the first real test. Forecast Confidence is capped at Medium for this reason alone.

## 9. Management Capital Allocation

| Dimension | Assessment |
|---|---|
| Payout policy | **Clear and public.** 75% of profit attributable to shareholders for FY2025, guided "stable-to-rising" for FY2026. Ratio has been ratcheted deliberately from ~65% (FY2021). |
| Reinvestment discipline | **Strong.** Capex cut ~27% from the 2021 peak; 2026 guided down a further 9.5% to RMB 136.6bn, with the mix rotated toward computing power (+62.4%) and away from radio network. |
| Leverage | **Ultra-conservative.** Net cash RMB 130–276bn; debt/equity ~0.07. Arguably over-capitalised. |
| Acquisitions | No material value-destructive M&A identified. |
| Ordinary issuance / ATM | **None** since the Dec 2021 A-share IPO. |
| Scrip / DRIP | **Not offered** — no dilution channel. |
| Shareholder returns | Dividends only. **Zero buybacks, ever.** |

**The one genuine criticism.** China Mobile sat on a RMB 200bn+ net cash pile while its own shares traded at roughly 4x earnings and a ~8.6% yield in 2021–2022, and repurchased nothing. A buyback at those levels would have been among the most value-accretive uses of capital available to any large-cap globally. Instead the company chose to lift the payout ratio — a decision that returns cash but consumes the very lever it now needs for future DPS growth.

There is also a subtler point worth flagging: the payout ratio is being lifted at the same time earnings have stopped growing. That is a legitimate response to maturity, but it means recent DPS growth has been *funded by policy rather than performance*, and the framework requires that to be normalised out of N. It is (see Section 15).

**Score: 12 / 15.** Clear policy, genuine discipline, zero dilution — but a demonstrable failure to use an obvious buyback window, and DPS growth increasingly dependent on a finite ratio lever.

**Normalization adjustments made:**

1. **Payout ratio normalized to 77%, below the Base-case path of 78–80%.** The Base case assumes management continues ratcheting the ratio to defend DPS growth. That is a plausible *policy* assumption, but it is not sustainable operating capacity — a payout ratio can be lifted only once, and the framework requires non-repeatable inputs to be stripped out of N. Using the Base-case 79% average would have produced N = HKD 5.00 and inflated every boundary by ~2.7%.
2. **FY2025's depressed OCF was not used to reduce N**, because DPS is policy-linked to *profit*, not to cash flow. Cash flow constrains coverage (tested separately in Section 14), not the DPS calculation.
3. **No cycle premium exists to remove.** This is not a commodity, freight, credit or rate-sensitive business; there is no windfall embedded in FY2025.

Because N is built from an explicit normalized distributable-capacity chain (normalized profit → normalized payout policy → fixed share count → withholding), the basis is `mid_cycle` rather than the lower-confidence `three_year_base_average`.

### Forecast assumptions and methodology

- **FX:** RMB 0.91 per HKD used throughout. The company's own FY2025 payout-ratio arithmetic implies ~0.904; the cash-flow statement settlement implies ~0.924. 0.91 is the midpoint. FX is modelled as a **transient** sensitivity driver and therefore does **not** move N or the buy-zone boundaries.

- **B source:** Bear-case sustained FY2027–28 distributable capacity at the 75% policy floor. Not an arbitrary haircut.
- **Valuation mode:** `ordinary_yield_based`. Structural Decline and the harvest exception do not apply.
- **Sensitivity classification:** four drivers classified `persistent`, one (`FX`) classified `transient`. No `structural` driver identified, so no model rebuild is required.
- **Future DPS status:** **evidence-backed**, derived from a public payout policy applied to reported and forecast profit over a fixed share count — not illustrative.
