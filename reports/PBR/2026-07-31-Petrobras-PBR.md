## 5. Dividend Treatment — the single most important change this year

**Takeaway:** Brazil ended three decades of tax-free dividends on 1 January 2026, and separately raised the JCP rate. Petrobras pays in JCP form, so the HK investor's rate went **15% → 17.5%**, permanently and with no relief available.

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Withholding rate applied | **17.5%** (JCP / *juros sobre capital próprio*) |
| Legal basis | **Complementary Law 224/2025** raised JCP IRRF from 15% → 17.5%, effective 1 Jan 2026 |
| Alternative rate | **10%** if paid as ordinary dividends — **Law 15,270/2025**, effective 1 Jan 2026 (previously 0%) |
| Which applies | Petrobras paid **100% JCP** for 4Q25 and 1Q26. Assume 17.5% |
| Withholding basis | `company_announcement` + `legal_structure` |
| Broker-observed | **Unknown** — no PBR line in the observed-withholding register (currently 0941.HK, 1919.HK only) |
| Broker cash-line type | Unknown — verify it is a dividend line, not PIL |
| Treaty relief | **None.** No Brazil–HK DTA, no Brazil–US DTA. HK does not tax foreign dividends → **no foreign tax credit is possible**. The 17.5% is a pure, permanent loss |
| ADR depositary fee | ~$0.01–0.02 per ADR per event, additional |
| Scrip / DRIP | Not available. All-cash election assumed |
| Withholding Efficiency | **Low** |

**Reconciliation check (validates the 17.5% assumption).** 1Q26 declared R$0.70097/share = **R$1.4019/ADR**. At FX 5.07 that is **$0.2765 gross**; less 17.5% = $0.2281; less ~$0.010 depositary fee = **$0.2254**. The actual ADR cash line was **$0.225356**. The match is essentially exact — and it confirms that the "$0.96 annual dividend / 5.02% yield" quoted by data providers is already a **net-of-tax** figure, not gross.

**Two flags worth acting on:**

1. **Hong Kong is on Brazil's favoured-taxation ("tax haven") list** (RFB IN 1037). JCP is legally *interest*, not a dividend, and interest remitted to blacklisted jurisdictions is subject to **25% IRRF**. For ADRs, withholding is normally applied at the **depositary's** level of residence (US), which should preserve the 17.5% rate — and the reconciliation above supports that. But this is the single assumption most worth verifying against a real broker statement. **If 25% applied, normalized net yield falls from 4.3% to 3.9%.** It is also an argument for the ADR over direct B3 shares.
2. **PBR.A is the better income instrument.** Identical DPS, price $16.85 vs $19.12 → net yield **5.71% vs 5.02%**, a 69bp pickup for the same cash flow and same tax. Every buy-zone boundary below should be multiplied by **0.881** for PBR.A.

## 6. Business Fundamentals and Long-Term Trend

**Dividend funding engine:** Pre-salt upstream cash flow — Búzios, Tupi and Mero — converted into distributable cash by a mechanical 45%-of-FCF formula. Everything else (refining, gas, renewables) is secondary to the payout.

**Takeaway:** Production is at all-time records and running ahead of plan; free cash flow has nonetheless halved since 2022 because capex tripled. Volume growth is not reaching the shareholder.

|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 2021 | ~$71 | 2.77 | 83.8 | 41.4% | 19.8 | 31.3 |
| 2022 | ~$99 | 2.68 | 124.3 | 47.3% | 36.5 | 39.9 |
| 2023 | ~$82 | 2.78 | 102.5 | 39.8% | 25.0 | 31.1 |
| 2024 | ~$80 | 2.70 | 91.0 | 31.8% | 6.8 | 23.0 |
| 2025 | ~$69 | 3.00 | 89.5 | 32.5% | 19.6 | 16.5 |

*USD converted from reported BRL at annual average FX; FY2025 confirmed against company-reported USD (OCF $36.0bn, capex $20.3bn, FCF $16.5bn, adj. EBITDA $43.8bn).*

**Fundamental Trend: Cyclical Peak / Normalization Risk.** Evidence:

- **Volume — genuinely strong.** Q2 2026 production **3.34 mmboed, +14.1% y/y**, a record. FPSO P-79 (Búzios 8, 180 kbpd) started **five months ahead of plan** on 1 May 2026. The company is already at the 3.4 mmboed level the 2026–2030 plan targeted for **2028–29**. Búzios installed capacity now ~1.33 mmbpd.
- **Price — at a premium that is unlikely to persist.** Brent averaged $80.61 in Q1 2026, spiked above **$120 in April** on Middle East conflict, and is back to **$83.59** (28 Jul). Goldman sees $56 average 2026 on a 2 mmbpd surplus; J.P. Morgan $80 in Q4; S&P Global $87. The dispersion is the point. Petrobras's own plan assumes **$63 (2026) and $70 (2027–30)**.
- **Capital intensity — structurally higher.** Capex $6.3bn (2021) → $20.3bn (2025) → ~$21–22bn/yr under the **$109bn 2026–2030 plan** ($91bn implementation target, $81bn base; $69.2bn to E&P). Net-debt-neutral Brent breakeven **$59/bbl in 2026**, falling to $48 by 2030.
- **FX — a live margin squeeze.** The plan assumes **R$5.80/USD**; actual is **5.07**. Revenue is USD-linked, a large share of costs, royalties and special participation is BRL. A ~13% stronger BRL than plan is worth roughly $3bn/yr of USD FCF.
- **Politics — the October 2026 general election.** Petrobras is selling fuel **below international parity** to contain election-year inflation, with a R$1.50/litre diesel subsidy in play, and is evaluating a **re-entry into fuel retailing** at the President's urging. Precedent is unambiguous: the CEO was removed in 2022 over pump prices.

**Core drivers selected:** (1) Brent, (2) production volume, (3) capex execution, (4) USD/BRL, (5) domestic fuel pricing discretion.

**Per-share dilution:** None. No issuance, no scrip, no ATM. Buybacks trivial (R$1.9bn in 2024, none since). Share count flat — a real positive versus most high-payout peers.

**Takeaway:** This is not a progressive dividend and has never claimed to be — it is a pass-through of a volatile FCF number, and it has fallen in four of the last five years.

**Dividend Pattern.** Petrobras runs a *formula*, not a *promise*. Because the formula caps the payout at 45% of free cash flow, coverage is structurally strong in every state of the world — including bad ones. The corollary is that the shareholder absorbs the full amplitude of Brent, FX and capex swings directly in the dividend. A 5-year DPS series that runs 2.06 → 5.77 → 3.01 → 1.23 → 0.99 is the honest signature of that design. It is *safe* in the sense that it will not be funded with debt; it is *unreliable* in the sense that no year predicts the next.

## 9. Management Capital Allocation

**Positives:** The remuneration policy is explicit, formula-based, published, and — verifiably — followed. FY2025 remuneration of $7,507m equals **45.5%** of FY2025 FCF of $16.5bn. The 1Q26 advance of R$9.03bn equals **~45%** of Q1 FCF of $3.9bn. No dilution in five years. Debt reduced every year. The plan trimmed the implementation portfolio by $7bn versus the prior plan in response to weaker prices — evidence of genuine discipline, not just language.

**Negatives:** Capex has tripled since 2021 and the shareholder has funded all of it out of foregone dividends. $15.8bn is directed to refining/petrochemicals/fertilisers and $13bn to energy transition across the plan — segments where Petrobras's historical returns have been poor and where the state's industrial-policy objectives and the minority shareholder's objectives diverge. A **$1.2bn renewable fuels plant** and a proposed re-entry into fuel *retailing* are both textbook examples. Selling fuel below import parity in an election year is an unlegislated transfer from shareholders to voters. And the board is government-appointed.

**Assessment:** the payout mechanism is trustworthy; the numerator feeding it is not fully under shareholder-aligned control.

**Bottom line, separating fact from judgment.**

*Facts:* Production is at record levels and running ahead of plan. Free cash flow has halved since 2022 as capex tripled. The 45% payout formula has been applied faithfully and every distribution has been funded from operating cash flow with no dilution. Brazil began withholding tax on both dividends (10%) and JCP (17.5%) on 1 January 2026, and Petrobras pays in JCP form. The shares are up ~45% over twelve months while the dividend fell.

*Assumptions:* Mid-cycle Brent $70, USD/BRL 5.30, capex $21.5bn/yr, payout maintained at 45%, share count flat, and Brazilian withholding applied at the depositary level so the HK investor faces 17.5% rather than the 25% tax-haven rate.

*Judgment:* Petrobras is a better-run and more shareholder-honest company than its reputation implies, and it is not a value trap. That is not compensation — it is a bet on the oil price wearing an income-stock costume. **Watchlist, not a buy.** If the exposure is wanted at some point, use **PBR.A**, which pays the identical dividend at an 11.9% lower price.
