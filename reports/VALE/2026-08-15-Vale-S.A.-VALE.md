## 5. Dividend Treatment — the number that has quietly changed

**Takeaway:** Brazil ended three decades of tax-free dividends on 1 January 2026 *and* raised the JCP rate in the same reform. Because Vale pays mostly in JCP form, the HK investor's blended rate went from roughly **0–15% to ~15.8%**, and it will stay there.

| Item | Treatment |
|---|---|
| Ordinary dividends, profits **accrued and approved by 31 Dec 2025** | **0%** — grandfathered under Law 15.270/2025 |
| Ordinary dividends, 2026 onwards | **10%** withholding (Law 15.270/2025) |
| JCP (juros sobre capital próprio) credited in 2025 | **15%** |
| JCP credited from 1 Jan 2026 | **17.5%** |
| Jul 2026 declaration mix | 77.25% JCP / 22.75% dividend → **blended 15.79%** |
| ADR depositary fee | **$0.005 per ADR per event** (~$0.010/yr) |
| **Effective all-in drag, go-forward** | **~17.0%** of gross → **net factor ≈ 0.83** |
| Treaty relief | **None.** Brazil and Hong Kong have no double-tax agreement |
| Refund mechanism | Law 15.270 allows a credit where company ETR + 10% > 34%, claimable within 360 days of year-end. **Not realistically accessible to a retail HK holder through an ADR** — treat as zero |
| Scrip / DRIP | Not available. All-cash assumed |
| **Withholding Efficiency** | **Low** |

The March 2026 line is the proof of the grandfathering: Vale paid a dividend slice *and* a JCP slice from the same November 2025 declaration, and the depositary withheld **exactly 15% on the JCP portion and nothing on the dividend portion**. That is the last time the investor will see a 0% line.

**Three flags worth acting on:**

1. **The September 2026 payment is the first true post-reform payment.** The tax figure above is an estimate. Check the actual Citi notice in early September and, if it prints materially above ~16%, revisit §15.
2. **The HK tax-haven question.** Brazil's IN 1037 regime applies **25%** to interest-type payments (which is what JCP legally is) made to privileged-regime jurisdictions, and Hong Kong's status is genuinely contested. The mitigating fact is that **the depositary withholds one rate for the entire ADR programme**, and the observed rate in Sep 2025 and Mar 2026 was exactly the standard 15% — not 25%. This report assumes programme-level standard rates. It remains the highest-value open question, and §11 sizes it.
3. **Published yield figures for VALE mix gross and net.** Some aggregators quote the Citi *gross rate*, others the *net rate*, in the same table. Every dividend number in this report is stated explicitly as gross or net.

Vale's C1 sits near the very bottom of the global cost curve, where the marginal seaborne tonne costs roughly $70–90/t. **This is why the correct classification is Cyclical Peak, not Structural Decline** — Vale survives a $70/t world; a large slice of the industry does not.

**Fundamental Trend: `Cyclical Peak / Normalization Risk`.** Evidence:

- *Price.* 62% Fe averaged ~$103/t over the last 12 months and ~$95/t in 2026 to date. Consensus for 2027 is **$82–87/t** and 2028 **$80–85/t**.
- *Supply.* **Simandou** shipped its first cargoes in late 2025, is heading for 50–60 Mtpa through 2027 and 120 Mtpa by 2028–30. It is the largest new high-grade supply source in three decades and breaks the Australia–Brazil duopoly.
- *Demand.* Chinese steel output is plateauing to declining; property remains the structural drag.
- *Copper is also at a peak.* Vale's realized copper price was $11,003/t in FY2025 and **$14,062/t in Q2 2026, +57% y/y**. Base Metals EBITDA rose ~80% y/y to ~$1.3bn in the quarter. That is a genuine business improvement *layered on top of* an extraordinary price.
- *Costs are moving the wrong way.* C1 guidance was raised from ~$20.0–21.5/t to $22.5–23.5/t and all-in from ~$52–56 to $58–62 on BRL strength and diesel.
- *Offsets.* Volumes at their best since 2018, capex guided down to $5.4–5.7bn, copper/nickel unit costs collapsing, 30+ year reserve life.

**Core drivers selected:** (1) iron ore realized price, (2) copper realized price, (3) iron ore C1 / all-in cost, (4) BRL/USD, (5) Samarco + Brumadinho cash claim.

**Per-share effects:** no issuance, no scrip, no DRIP, no ATM. Share count has only ever gone down. Weighted-average basic shares 4,840m (2021) → 4,270m (2025), **−11.8%**, so per-share economics have consistently beaten headline economics.

## 9. Management Capital Allocation

- **Payout policy.** Since August 2025, a published formula: **minimum 30% × (Adjusted EBITDA − sustaining capex)**, in two instalments (September, March), with board discretion to add extraordinary remuneration. This is one of the more honest policies in the sector — it is defined on a cash-relevant measure and it is verifiable. The 1H26 declaration of $1.70bn reconciles to the formula almost exactly.
- **What the July 2026 decision tells you.** Iron ore volumes were at a post-2018 record, copper was at an all-time high, Q2 FCF was up 49% — and the board still paid **only the minimum** and simultaneously authorised a new buyback. That is a board that thinks expanded net debt near $16.7bn, inside a $10–20bn band, with an R$100bn Samarco schedule running for 20 years, does not leave room for generosity.
- **Reinvestment.** Capex has been *cut*, from an initial $6.5bn plan to **$5.4–5.7bn**, with a stated long-term ceiling below $6bn. Growth capex is being concentrated in copper. This is rational: copper all-in cost fell from $2,616/t to $603/t.
- **Leverage.** Net debt has risen every year since 2021 but from a near-zero base; expanded net debt has been range-bound at $14–16.5bn. Investment grade, no refinancing wall identified.
- **The legacy claim.** Brumadinho and Samarco are not provisions on paper — they are cash. Actual disbursements from Vale's own FCF waterfall:

**The honest tension in this table.** On its own five-year price history, VALE is unremarkable — $13.63 against a five-year average of roughly $13.08 and a five-year range midpoint of ~$14.28. It gives no credit to copper volume growth, to the option value of the new 100m-share buyback, to Vale's cost-curve position as a survival asset in a downturn, or to any re-rating of the equity. An investor whose objective is total return rather than after-tax cash income should reach a materially less negative conclusion than this report does, and should say so explicitly rather than borrowing this verdict.
